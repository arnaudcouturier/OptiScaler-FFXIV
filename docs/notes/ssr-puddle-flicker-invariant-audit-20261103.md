# SSR and puddle flicker: invariant audit (2026-11-03)

Scope: source at `74fa3fdc66b787fb4cfbef5959acbfbce75d426a` plus the existing diagnostic edits in this worktree, and captures under `../analysis/telemetry-20261103-1705-1707`. The diagnostic edits are preserved. This is an invariant audit, not a visual root-cause verdict; the captures do not include per-frame images of the inputs and output.

## Findings

1. **The sampled SSR frames were served; skip/refusal is not the explanation for their flicker.** In `20261103-170729-4/frames.csv`, all 16 rows report `ssr_valid=1`, `ssr_served=8`, `ssr_runs=1`, `ssr_declines=0`. The other sampled bursts also show valid SSR runs (some were captured before puddle readers appeared). `engine.txt` reports 12,120 cumulative runs, 109,945 readers, and zero declines. DLSS-G being off is consistent with this evidence and does not change the pipeline invariants below. A valid/served counter proves the output was bound, not that the geometry or color it contains was temporally aligned.

2. **The SSR pass has a real source-order/temporal invariant to verify: one frame token is tied to the first qualifying water draw, and camera, scene, and V-position inputs are captured at that draw.** `SsrApply.cpp` uses `FrameCount()` and `FrameCache::Begin()` to permit one attempt per output frame; `SsrPass.cpp` copies the bound camera table, then copies the currently bound scene render target's render window into its private scene texture, copies the planar map, and dispatches the march. The captured `g_SamplerVPosition` remains the buffer bound on that water draw. This is internally ordered on the immediate context, so the copy commands precede the SSR draw; no source evidence here shows a stale private camera/scene buffer being read ahead of its queued copy. However, the cache does not independently prove that the camera constant buffer, V-position buffer, and scene target all describe the same *render sample* when earlier game/NR stages have substituted or advanced one of them. The counter measures output presents, not a scene-render identity. The capture has no per-water-draw binding history or image hashes to establish that stronger equality. Treat this as an unresolved alignment risk, not a confirmed defect.

3. **Projection/jitter is not demonstrated to be mismatched.** SSR consumes view-space positions and the copied camera table in its shader; it does not reconstruct V-position from a separate depth input. The puddle tone pass captures V-position, normal, camera and scene at the deferred composite and uses that captured set later at the tone-map stage. Both use allocation texels and their render-window origin/extent; SSR explicitly maps the planar map's smaller region to the scene allocation, whereas puddles operate at the scene/G-buffer allocation. The draw record `frames.csv` has only three `g_LightParam` rows and is not a camera/projection/jitter history. Therefore it cannot prove either matching or mismatch of projection jitter under DLSS-NR. Do not use the earlier frozen-G-buffer, projection-only perturbation as evidence: it changes projection while holding the corresponding scene buffers fixed, violating the input-set invariant itself.

4. **The puddle stages paired in the sampled wet run; their inputs can still change each frame.** In `20261103-170729-4/frames.csv`, each row has matching SSR/puddle state tokens (`ssr_token`, `puddle_gloss_token`, `wetness_token`, and tone capture token are one behind the sampling callback token), `puddle_gloss_valid=1`, `puddle_gloss_served=289`, `tone_capture_taken=1`, `tone_shape_wet=1`, one tone run, and zero tone declines. `engine.txt` shows one initial gloss camera-copy-not-ready event across the full capture, not recurring puddle skips. That first not-ready outcome is the intended asynchronous camera-layout check (`GlossPuddlesPass.cpp::CheckCamera`), not stale camera data used by a successful draw.

5. **Wetness is a live shape input, not a static enable bit.** The same 16-row telemetry window reports known wetness falling from about 0.2663 to 0.2505 while `wet_latched` stays true and gloss/tone continue to run. `NoteWetnessFrame` takes one GPU-side copy per output frame from the first draw with the engine wetness binding it can accept; `PuddleReflection.hlsl` multiplies the pool mask by wetness and thresholds its world-space noise field. Thus pool boundaries/intensity can move as the weather wetness changes even with both stages paired and no decline. This is a plausible contributor to visible wetness change, not proof it caused the reported puddle flicker. The captured `puddle pool share: 0.000` is not evidence that a floor sample is invalid; it only says the measured diagnostic statistic counted no pool texels in the capture window.

6. **Scene-copy ordering is explicit and state-restored, with one residual runtime caveat.** SSR unbinds OM render/depth views before copying the current scene target, restores them immediately, then copies the engine planar map and draws into its own output. Puddle gloss draws into a private shaped G-buffer target and is swapped only for the current reader; tone captures the composite inputs and later copies only its edge band before its full-window draw. These source paths do not reveal a straightforward copy-after-overwrite bug. D3D resource hazard/state semantics and the game's producer order still need a capture with stage/binding identity to settle across game versions; source comments themselves flag the bound-source copy as something runtimes/drivers may treat differently.

## Coordinate and sample interpretation

- SSR works on a region of the planar map whose dimensions are scaled from the scene allocation; its private scene copy uses scene-allocation coordinates and V-position is loaded at those corresponding scene texels. The water shader reads the replacement planar map in allocation-relative coordinates. This relationship is documented in `screen-space-reflections.md` and encoded in `SsrRules.h` (`RegionOf`, `SceneCopyOf`).
- Puddle gloss uses same-index G-buffer target/depth texels, and puddle tone uses same-index scene/V-position/normal texels, restricted to the render window. Camera inverse-view transforms those view-space positions to world space for the stable wetness field. A handful of floor values or a single texel's raw coordinate cannot by itself establish a bug: expected jitter, window offset, scale, normal decode, and the camera transform must be considered together.
- The puddle shader has an intentional real-time rain clock for splash rings (`PuddleToneDraw.cpp::RippleSeconds`, `PuddleReflection.hlsl`). This is another designed temporal input; it is distinct from the static world-anchored pool mask. If the reported wetness change continues with the rain/ripple component disabled, the clock is not its explanation.

## Conclusion / evidence needed

The captures establish successful service and stage pairing in the sampled intervals, and show gradual wetness variation. They do **not** establish a stale camera buffer, projection/jitter mismatch, or wrong scene-copy stage. The main unresolved common-input invariant is scene-render identity: a shared render sample (or a deliberate reprojection between samples) must underlie the copied camera, V-position/normal, and scene color. To prove a violation, capture per-render identity plus camera-buffer identity/hash, V-position and scene resource identities, viewport/window and jitter values at the SSR producer and each water reader; for puddles, include wetness source value and tone-stage capture identity. A matched frame dump of those resources is preferable to inferring from floor samples or pass counters.
